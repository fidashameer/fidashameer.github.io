Predictive vs. Reactive SDN Load Balancing Under Flash-Crowd Traffic
Negative result · IEEE-format paper
Python · Ryu · Mininet · OpenFlow · Open vSwitch

Application-layer service meshes (Envoy, Linkerd) use EWMA-smoothed latency scores to bias traffic toward faster backends, and it works well there. The question: does the same technique still help once you move it down a layer, into an SDN controller that only sees OpenFlow port-statistics instead of real response latencies?

I implemented four load-balancing controllers in Ryu over a Mininet/Open vSwitch testbed and measured P95/P99 tail latency across seven or more independent runs per configuration, over homogeneous and heterogeneous (1:2:4) server pools, under burst and steady traffic, plus an α-sensitivity sweep.

P99 tail latency, ms — mean, with ± SD whiskers
Stable (SD < 5ms) Unstable (SD > 15ms) Whiskers = ±1 SD across runs

Round-robin and weighted round-robin are both the fastest and the most stable. EWMA's standard deviation runs 14–28× larger than round-robin's — not just "no better on average," but markedly less predictable run to run.

α-sensitivity sweep — heterogeneous, burst (hover a point)

Moderate smoothing (α 0.1–0.7) sits in a comparable band; at α = 0.9 the balancer thrashes and P99 collapses to ~240ms.

The answer turned out to be no: EWMA never beats plain round-robin, in any tested condition, and is an order of magnitude less stable. The mechanism is straightforward once you see it — EWMA's smoothing is designed to reject transient noise, but a persistently slow server isn't noise, it's a stable signal that needs an immediate reaction. The property that would help during a bursty, homogeneous flash crowd is exactly the property that hurts under sustained, heterogeneous load. I wrote it up as a negative result with a mechanistic explanation rather than dressing it up as a win.
